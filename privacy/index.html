Privacy Policy

Last updated: 17 November 2026 · Contact: support@tellsy.app.

In short. Tellsy has no account, no server, no advertising, no tracking and no analytics. Your dictations and items stay on your iPhone, or go to Apple's own apps if you choose. The developer never receives them, so items kept only in Tellsy cannot be recovered if you delete the app or lose your iPhone without a backup. Apple's speech recognition may process audio on Apple's servers when a language is not available on the device. Purchases are processed by Apple; the developer receives no payment or personal data.

Text understanding and translation

Tellsy understands commands with rules that run on your iPhone. Smart understanding is off by default. If you enable it in Tellsy Settings and Apple Intelligence is available, it uses only Apple's on-device Foundation Models framework. When the model is unavailable or fails, the app uses its rules. There is no cloud AI fallback.

Translation is also off by default. If you select a translation language, Apple's Translation framework processes the text on your device. iOS may ask to download language packs. If a language pair is unsupported or translation fails, the app keeps the original text. Tellsy has no third-party AI integration, API-key setting, advertising, tracking or analytics.

Voice dictation and Apple services

Tellsy requests microphone and speech-recognition permission when you start recording. It streams audio to Apple's Speech framework, requiring on-device recognition when Apple supports it for the language. Otherwise, Apple may process audio and recognition context through its speech service under Apple's privacy policy. The developer does not receive audio, transcripts or recognition context. Tellsy does not save audio recordings.

Siri and Apple Watch system dictation are Apple features governed by your system settings and Apple's policies. Commands can also be typed. You can revoke microphone and speech permissions in iOS Settings without losing access to typed commands or existing items.

Contacts, Calendar and Reminders

Contacts access is optional, including access to only the contacts you select. Tellsy uses permitted contacts to match names you mention. Matched email addresses and phone numbers can be added to an event's notes. If Use contact names is enabled, accessible first names are supplied as recognition context to Apple's speech recognizer. Contact matching itself runs on your iPhone. You can disable contact matching and contact vocabulary separately in Tellsy Settings, or revoke Contacts access in iOS Settings.

Calendar and Reminders permissions are requested when needed to create or edit your items. Notes and lists are exported using the share sheet or a Shortcut you configure. With the share sheet, or with the “Save to Notes” Shortcut that Tellsy provides, the text goes to Apple Notes on your device, and that Shortcut tells Tellsy when the note has been made. If you build a Shortcut of your own that takes the text from Tellsy, the text goes wherever your Shortcut sends it, including any other app or service you put in it; Tellsy cannot see or control that, does not treat the note as delivered, and shows it in History as “Returned to Shortcut, not confirmed” until you confirm it yourself. Alternatively, choose Tellsy only to keep items within the app. Exported items may sync through iCloud or another account configured in Apple's apps; those copies are controlled by the receiving app and account.

Purchases

Tellsy is free to download and includes a free 10-day trial of every feature. After the trial, recording new items needs a one-time purchase, Tellsy Pro. Purchases are processed by Apple through the App Store and belong to your Apple Account. The developer has no server and receives no payment details, no card number, no name, no email address and no Apple Account identifier. Apple's handling of purchases is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

So that the app can answer the moment it opens, and Siri and the Apple Watch respect the trial, Tellsy keeps a small status record on your iPhone, in a storage area shared only between the app and its own widget: the date your trial started, the date and identifier of Apple's record of that trial purchase, whether Tellsy Pro is owned, and the latest time the app has seen (so that changing the clock neither lengthens the trial nor starts it again). Only the app reads this record. The Today widget and the Lock Screen control do not read it: they open the app, and the app decides. It is worked out from Apple's signed record of your purchases on the device, is protected in the same way as History, is never sent anywhere, and is removed when you delete the app. Restore Purchases in Settings asks the App Store for your purchases again; your trial's start date and Tellsy Pro come back with your Apple Account, not from the developer. A sentence dictated on the Apple Watch while your iPhone is out of reach is kept in the Watch app's own storage until the iPhone confirms that it has written it down, and is then removed from the Watch. If such a sentence reaches your iPhone while Tellsy is locked after the trial, it is held on the iPhone (up to 50 sentences; any more stay on the Watch until there is room), protected like History, and turned into an item once Tellsy is unlocked; it is removed when you delete the app.

Storage, retention and deletion

The app stores text transcripts, items, settings and recognition vocabulary in its own storage. History is protected by device file protection and can require Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode to read. Lock Screen transcript display is off by default and can be enabled separately. Device backups may contain app data according to your backup settings.

Reminders and meetings kept in Tellsy are announced by notifications that the app schedules on the device; nothing is sent to a server to deliver them. A notification carries the reminder's or meeting's title, and whether that title shows on a locked iPhone is decided by iOS (Settings › Notifications › Tellsy › Show Previews). For the Today widget, the app keeps a short summary of the coming week's meetings and reminders (titles and times; times only while My items is locked) in a storage area shared only with its own widget, protected in the same way as History. It is kept up to date whether or not the widget is on screen, and deleting the app removes it.

You can delete individual or filtered History items, clear all History, forget learned vocabulary and delete custom vocabulary in the app. Sent items expire after the retention period you select (90 days by default, with options of 30 days, one year or forever). Awaiting, failed and kept items are not automatically removed, and neither is a note returned to a Shortcut and not confirmed. If History, or part of it, cannot be read, the app keeps a recovery copy of the unreadable entries on your iPhone, next to History and protected in the same way, and History shows a notice that says so. You can delete that copy from the notice (Delete the copy) or with History › Delete… › Delete everything, which is available even when no entries are shown; deleting the app removes its entire local storage, the copy included. Backups and items already exported to another app must be managed separately in their respective apps or accounts.

Turn off Smart understanding or Translation in Tellsy Settings to stop those optional features. The developer has no account or server containing your items and cannot recover or remotely delete them. For privacy questions, email support@tellsy.app; do not include private dictations unless you wish to share them in that email.

If you delete the app or lose your iPhone

Items you keep only in Tellsy (the “Tellsy only” choice under Settings › Where things go) are stored only on your iPhone. They are not synced to iCloud or to any server, and the developer has no copy of them. If you delete the app, reset your iPhone, or the iPhone is lost, stolen or damaged, those items and your History are permanently lost and cannot be recovered, unless you can restore them from an iPhone backup (iCloud Backup or a backup on your computer) made before the loss.

Items you send to Apple's Calendar, Reminders or Notes are kept by those apps and, if you use iCloud for them, synced to your other devices and recoverable through your Apple account. For anything you cannot afford to lose, send it to the Apple app or make sure iCloud Backup is turned on.
